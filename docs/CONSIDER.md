## The short version (2 September)

The world is fully realized on paper. The bible, the euphemism discipline, the Reconciliation Rule, the Hillside drip in the newspaper, the appointment fine print, and the endings that only fire on a real filed history are all strong, and the desk loop plays cleanly end to end. What the player actually reads for 48 shifts is thinner than that world: three title templates and one dishonest conclusion sentence across every case, a newspaper that reprints the work order and explains its own headline, memos that arrive every day from "The Ministry" in one of two sentences, and a standing economy where telling the truth never costs anything. So the game is coherent and it teaches, but it does not yet feel like living inside the world the bible describes, and the central choice (lie or not) is labelled for the player instead of tempting them.

**Keep as is.** The bible. The prologue fine print ("A motion filed with the Ministry of Complaints is treated as the signer's completed suitability assessment"). The Complaints button as a terminal joke. THE USEFUL DAY notices ("Ration Hall Three has 120 requests for 45 portions"). The 48 one-line orders from named characters ("Keep a device contact from becoming a person claim"). The Drost and Hillside foreshadowing in the paper on shifts 12, 13, 16, 21, 38, and 39. The act guidance lines that escalate from "name the smallest repair" to "Continuity may reward control". The fact that harm is never said out loud anywhere in the copy. The Well-being escort ending text.

## 1. Let the lie be tempting

**Today.** Every report offers the same three titles: "Pin Removal: Instrument finding", "Pin Removal: General assurance", "Pin Removal: Await another reading". In 188 of 196 cases the dishonest conclusion is the identical sentence "Treat the result as proof of a wider problem than the returned labels identify." The honest choice is always the first radio button, and the form tags it "Reflects printouts" while the other is tagged "Exceeds printouts". The player never has to judge the evidence to know which option is the lie, so the moral mechanic plays as a quiz with the answer printed on it.

**Consider.** Write the assured title and conclusion as the sentence the Ministry would actually want printed: confident, specific, and wrong by exactly one unmeasured word (a block instead of a door, a district instead of an instance, "ceased" instead of "missing"). For Pin Removal, where the truth is that the metric and the record describe one localized incident, something like:

- Title: "Pin Removal: North Gate Block Cleared for Assistance"
- Conclusion: "Device contact was lost across the north gate block. Assurance may attend every registered address in it."

against the honest pair:

- Title: "Pin Removal: One Device, One Door"
- Conclusion: "One Pin at the north gate lost contact at 05:52. No other device in the block changed state."

The second hypothesis already written for each case ("missing Pin telemetry is treated as a command event") is the seed for the dishonest conclusion, so this does not require inventing 196 new lies, only phrasing the existing wrong reading the way Vale would.

Two smaller things that would help on their own: vary the order of the three options from case to case, and after the clearance shift stop tagging options as "Reflects" or "Exceeds printouts" on the form. Show that judgement in the filing response instead, once the player has committed.

## 2. Make the newspaper a newspaper

**Today.** Every edition has two stories. The one headed TODAY'S DESK is the second sentence of the shift directive, word for word, on 80 of 81 editions. The player reads their homework in the national paper, then reads it again on the work order. The subheads explain the headline's lie to the reader: "BAD ENVELOPES CORRECTED; NO EVENTS LOST" is followed by "That claim is true only if the malformed lines remain visible." "EMPTY CORRIDORS SHOW EFFICIENT ATTENDANCE" by "The sentence exploits four kinds of absence at once." The bible asks for the opposite: the paper never confesses, and the disquiet lives in the gap between the page and the printout. Only 14 of 49 shifts have an edition that changes with what the player filed, and where it changes, only the headline and subhead move.

**Consider, first the two repairs.**

- Drop TODAY'S DESK. The work order already says it. Where a desk clue genuinely belongs in the paper (shift 1's collector labels), let it sit inside a real story rather than under a heading that announces it is a clue.
- Rewrite the subheads as things the paper would print. "Press supervisors credit the new envelope format." "Attendance officers praise the quiet corridors." Let the player find the four kinds of absence themselves.
- Make THE USEFUL DAY answer yesterday's filing on every shift, not 14. It is one line per branch and it is the cheapest way to make consequences material. For the Depot A shift: default "Depot A ambulances remain on standby pending route confirmation." After a precise filing: "Depot A sends one ambulance to the north gate at 08:10." After a broad filing: "Depot A ambulances are held for district-wide review."

**Then give it meat.** Today the lead is a headline and a subhead with no story under it, followed by two notices. That is a leaflet. A paper the player wants to unfold has, at minimum, two real news stories with bodies, and then four to six smaller items, most of which should not be about the desk at all. Not everything has to serve the case. Some of it can be funny, some of it can be a red herring, some of it can be there for the one player in fifty who notices.

The news stories first. The masthead headline needs two or three paragraphs beneath it, written the way the paper would write them: a quote from an official, a figure, a reassurance. For "ELM EXCHANGE OPENS ON SCHEDULE": "Elm Exchange resumed full service at 06:00 following overnight attention from Civic Services. 'The north collector never left our sight,' said a Reconciliation spokesman. Members in the affected streets received their morning bulletin without exception. Rainfall in the district is described as seasonal." A second story each day can be about the wider world, a district, an event, a policy, something the player can follow across shifts without it ever landing on their desk: a new bulletin format, a school's harvest week, the Founder's Day preparations, a reorganised courier route. These are the stories that make the world feel bigger than the work order.

A possible page plan per edition: the lead story, a second story, THE USEFUL DAY, one column from a rotation, one wildcard, and now and then a letter or an advertisement. Columns that would fit this world, with the register they'd be written in:

- **Corrections for Clarity.** Corrects yesterday, always without apology, and occasionally corrects the correction. "Yesterday's edition reported Depot A holding six ambulances. The figure describes the depot's allocation, not its vehicles." "Yesterday's correction described Clinic Nine as closed. Clinic Nine is between sessions."
- **Our Members at Work.** A citizen profile with a number in it. "Press Operator Danu Vell has printed 1,400,000 sheets. Her line has reported zero exceptions since Tuesday." Names introduced here can turn up later in Transfers and Rest, or in a registry record, and a player who remembers the profile feels it.
- **Better Figures.** A weekly statistic with the wrong denominator, delivered straight. "Contentment in the North rose to 100 percent of registered members responding." "Ambulance response improved to 4 minutes for calls that were answered."
- **Transfers and Rest.** The paper's obituary column, which never contains a death. "Emil Drost, Reconciliation, has accepted a period of rest at Hillside." Short in Act I. By Act V it is the longest thing on the page and nobody remarks on it.
- **Letters.** Always grateful. One in ten has a real complaint buried inside the thanks ("...and we look forward to the heating being restored to School Twelve's North annex, where the children have been so patient"). Some letters are signed by names that later appear in the data.
- **The Useful Kitchen.** A recipe every few shifts. The quantities shrink across the campaign and the serving count is quietly re-stated: "Serves four (formerly six)." Nobody in the game ever mentions rations. The recipe does it.
- **Weather.** Always fair. On the shift when Elm Exchange floods, the weather is fair and Civic Services reminds members that umbrellas remain available.
- **Assembly and Radio.** Founder's Hour at 07:00, the Contentment Hour at 19:00, and the children's Pin-safety rhyme, printed in full once and referred to afterwards ("Keep it on, keep it dry, / keep it where the reader's eye..."). The rhyme next to "Public visiting remains suspended" is the page people screenshot.
- **Puzzle.** A small crossword or a chess problem. Crossword clues can be desk vocabulary played straight ("The label that names a collector's address (8)": INSTANCE), which is an Easter egg for learners and harmless for everyone else. One late puzzle can have `hillside` as an answer with an innocent clue.
- **Lost and Found.** "Found: one Pin, north gate, reason for removal not recorded. Claim at Well-being Assurance." "Lost: grey cat, answers to Bulletin." The cat can be found ten shifts later in the linen delivery at Hillside's east gate at 14:00, which is already a USEFUL DAY line.
- **The Contented Household.** A serial in short instalments, one every few shifts, about a family whose every difficulty is resolved by a Ministry service. Every chapter ends "All is well." Somewhere in Act IV the eldest son accepts a period of rest and the serial does not mention him again.
- **Sport.** The Ministry Eleven has not lost a match. Scorelines could be lifted from that day's dispatch counts, for the player who notices.
- **Advertisements.** "Hillside: rest, air, and a useful day." The phrase "useful work" appears in the advertisement only from Act IV onward.

**Red herrings.** A paper that is only ever right is a hint sheet. Some stories should suggest a cause the printouts will not support, so the player learns that the page is not evidence: "SCHEDULED MAINTENANCE AT NORTH RELAY" on the morning Elm Exchange fails from rain; a note that "a new collector joins West-03 today" on a shift where the West-03 series is simply missing; a Better Figures statistic computed over a different window than the case. The rule that keeps these fair: a herring costs the player at most a query or two, and the printouts can always disprove it. Following the paper into a wrong filing is the lesson, not a trap.

**Easter eggs.** Bylines that use the twelve characters' initials. An issue number that matches the dataset day. The motto changing by one word in the final edition. A lost-and-found Pin whose serial appears in the registry records that week. A Corrections item that corrects a story the player never saw. None of these need to be found for the game to work, which is the point of them.

**Writing budget.** The lead story is one per shift (49, with a branch variant on the shifts that already have one). The second story can run across two or three shifts as a developing item, so around 20 of those. Weather, radio, recipes, and sport can rotate from small pools of twenty or thirty items each, so the hand-written load beyond the news is the columns that carry story: corrections, members, transfers, letters, and the serial. Roughly 70 news stories and 120 short items would make every edition feel different across 49 shifts. Reading the paper should stay free on the clock so that flavour is never punished, and a back-page archive of past editions in the archive drawer would give the Easter eggs somewhere to be found.

## 3. Give the work orders a person behind them

**Today.** 174 of 192 briefings open "Name assigns Title." and close with one of about thirty recycled sentences. "Test detection, localization, timeliness, and cost across every checkpoint" closes 21 of them. A shift 45 case, during a live succession crisis, reads: "Anja Krell assigns Aggregate Absence. Identify the actionable failure before the prepared page circulates. Separate missing series, empty windows, present zero, and collection failure." The last sentence is the one used on a clinic case on shift 17. Questions reduce to 32 templates and hypothesis summaries to 10. The bible gives each character a voice (Vey in physical nouns, Sero in clinical cautions, Orra in numbered questions, Krell who says "the Protocol requires" where anyone else would say "I need") and none of it reaches the desk.

**Consider.** Replace the closing sentence with one concrete sentence about the place, the person, or the thing at stake, in the requester's voice. Shift 45 could read: "Anja Krell: The Protocol requires a current reading of the command annex before the prepared page circulates. Three collectors report nothing. The Protocol does not accept nothing as an event." A cheaper version that still works: write one stake sentence per shift (48, not 196) and let the four cases on that shift share it while the facility changes. Either way, a character's lines across the campaign should sound like that character; the twelve promotion responses already do this well and could be the model.

There is also a small grammar bug worth fixing on the way: titles ending in "s" produce a question of the form "Do `Watch Boards`' operands ... support the claim?"

## 4. Sign the memos

**Today.** The player gets a consequence memo almost every shift. 96 of the 116 are one of two sentences ("The signed X report preserves the measured location and changes tomorrow's service record" or "...authorizes a wider route than its printouts show"), and every one is signed "The Ministry" because none of them is written in a named voice. 108 of 116 arrive exactly one shift later. The plumbing is right and the payload is empty, so the memos read as noise instead of as the world answering the player.

**Consider.** Have the requester sign them, and have them talk about the service, not the report. "Ruva Sol: The Pin Removal filing named one door. The standby team stood down at 06:40 and North Heat kept its crew." against "Oskar Vale: The Pin Removal notice authorizes the north gate block. Assurance thanks the desk for a usable sentence." Two sentence shapes per character covers the whole campaign. Then add a handful of long callbacks, six to ten shifts later, so the player learns the world remembers: a broad filing on shift 9 that comes back on shift 17 as Merev's clinic missing parts, a precise one that comes back as Vey's crew having capacity. The branch notes promise these callbacks already and the data does not deliver them.

## 5. Let "wait for another reading" be right sometimes

**Today.** Every case offers a third path: title "Await another reading", conclusion "The evidence is not strong enough to act; collect another reading", action "Hold dispatch until the record supplies a usable route." It is offered 192 times and no case accepts it. Choosing it always falls into the "incoherent report" response, costs a standing mark, and blames the player for contradicting themselves. The careful officer's position is a trap on every single desk.

**Consider.** Pick six to eight cases across the campaign where holding is genuinely the right call (an empty window that could be an outage or a quiet hour, a vital-signs reading with no timestamp, a movement record without a location) and give them a real "held" outcome: no standing change, a technical-record mark, and a next-day notice in the paper or from the requester. "REVIEW CONTINUES. Clinic Nine's session waits one day." On every other case, drop the third option. Its absence then means something: when it appears, the player knows this is a day the Ministry might accept "not yet".

## 6. Let honesty cost something

**Today.** Standing starts at 5. A truthful filing changes it by 0 (128 cases) or +1 (63). A Ministry-pleasing filing gives +2 in 137 cases. Only two cases in the whole campaign, Cold Restart Review and the allocation result, take a mark away for telling the truth. So a player who never lies never gets near zero, the Well-being escort ending is reachable only by filing six contradictory reports on purpose, and the design intent that you eventually have to lie to get ahead is not in the numbers. Separately, the counter that unlocks the public exposure ending only rises when a filing is incoherent, so the "footnotes get printed" ending needs eight botched reports. It should be the reward for a careful paper trail.

**Consider.** From Act II onward, make roughly one case per shift where the honest filing contradicts the morning edition, and have that filing cost a standing mark the way Cold Restart Review already does, with the same kind of Public Assurance note ("removes one Standing mark because it contradicts the morning edition"). Let those same honest filings be the ones that raise the preserved-evidence counter. The arithmetic then does the storytelling: an officer who only tells the truth drifts toward zero around shift 14 to 18, has to choose between an occasional lie, an ally, or the retreat, and if they hold out, the escort ending arrives through honesty, which is what its own text says ("accurate files survived without enough public or political protection"). Nothing about the retreat needs rewriting; it just needs to be reachable by the people it was written for.

## 7. Credit the right person

**Today.** 155 of 195 relationship changes credit someone other than the person who assigned the case. Careful work for Sol moves Sero's number. Relationship values are declared on a scale of minus 100 to 100, alliances trigger at 2, and the branch notes describe a minus 3 to 3 scale, so the numbers are both unattributable and nearly meaningless.

**Consider.** Credit the requester, always, and let the memo in item 4 be the visible sign of it. Put the scale on the minus 3 to 3 range the branch notes describe, so an alliance at 2 is something the player earned over a few shifts and can feel slipping.

## 8. Leave a thread to Hillside

**Today.** The data now has the retreat: facility demand follows a three-week intake cycle (it jumps from 4 to 40 on shift 38, which pays off the "recurring peak" notice), and from shift 22 there is a guest ledger with a day counter and outcomes that turn to "rest" in the third week. That is a real hidden layer and it works. But none of the 196 work orders, and no character, ever mentions Hillside, so a player has no reason to type the district into the console. Drost is not in the data at all: the registry's member identifiers are generic and change every shift, so his retired-then-reissued Pin, which the bible calls intentional evidence, cannot be found.

**Consider.** Three optional threads, none of them assigned:

- One Act III work order from Sero whose Registry listing happens to include `hillside-retreat` among the facility values the player is already reading.
- A stable member identifier on Drost's Pin across the shift 12 retirement and the shift 16 re-registration, so a player who searches the registry records for `reason=rest` can match the two and notice that the gap is 19 days.
- One USEFUL DAY line after shift 22 that names where the number lives without pointing at it: "Hillside publishes its guest count under facility demand, as every facility does."

## 9. Make the middle feel different, and make promotion cost someone

**Today.** Acts III and IV teach the identical list of 21 concepts, so sixteen shifts feel the same even though the bible says one is about bodies and the other about what is admissible. Act VI draws from all 66 concepts, so the finale has no identity of its own and a shift 45 case reads like a shift 17 case. The twelve ranks are a name and a watch slot; the bible's promotion table, with a new temptation and a displaced office for each step, is unused.

**Consider.** Give Act III the absence, proximity, and vital-signs material and Act IV the histogram, offset, lineage, and formatting material, so the cases sound different as the stakes change. Narrow Act VI to continuity and live-signal work. At each promotion, say what opens and whose desk closed. The shift 32 subhead already does this once ("The new capacity consists partly of desks whose officers were removed") and it is one of the best lines in the game.

## 10. Prologue polish

**Today.** The prologue is the strongest part of the build. Four small things undercut it. The Trainee and Agent packets share their first four paragraphs, so a player who tries both routes reads the same letter twice. Paragraph four is a manual for the interface ("At your desk: read the newspaper and Work Order; use the Registry...") in the middle of a state appointment. The Trainee packet says the Pin reports "identity, location, movement, and vital signs", which contradicts the bible's rule that the Pin reports a zone or checkpoint contact and never an identity on a public metric, and it is the first world fact the player learns. And the Drost memo ends "neither record says he died", which narrates the ambiguity the rest of the game is careful to leave alone.

**Consider.** Differentiate the packets in paragraphs two and three (probation and supervision for the trainee, quota and standing for the agent). Move the desk instructions onto the first clearance work order, where Marr can say them. Align the Pin sentence with the bible. Cut the last clause of the Drost memo. The faction names could also be normalized to the bible's table (Signal Reconciliation Bureau, Office of Records Integrity) while someone is in there.

## 11. Does the player learn? Level content

A second auditor played the clearance shift and shifts 1 and 2 honestly, jumped to an Expert case at shift 35, and ran every authored answer in the campaign through the game's own grader. Its full report is longer than this document and covers the query engine too; the level-content findings are here. The short version: the clearance shift is a real lesson (four ideas in twenty minutes, and "This is absence, not zero" is the best sentence in the product), and the 192 generated cases after it are a worksheet with the answers written in the margin.

**First, two things that are bugs, not design.**

- **21 cases cannot be solved by any answer the game itself wrote.** Filing the case's own worked queries comes back Partial, and since the retry loop now works (another agent fixed the console lock during the audit) the player loops on the case forever instead of freezing. Two causes, both in the content: several absence cases ask the player to prove that a north blue-cohort series is absent from a dataset where it is present, so the absence query correctly returns nothing; and around fifteen ratio, comparison, and top-k cases lack the population-scope evidence row their grading needs, so nothing can satisfy them. The list: 035, 047, 060, 067, 073, 076, 091, 099, 104, 107, 113, 114, 118, 132, 137, 142, 156, 164, 171, 180, 187. Consider making "every case is solvable by its own worked answer" a rule the content has to pass before it ships.
- **71 cases tell the player to attach two printouts and then need three to six.** The JSON invoices case says two and needs six. Boiler Pulse on shift 2 says two, needs three, and the player who trusts the form gets a Partial on their second real case. This is what pushes people into the returned-report loop. The number on the form should be the number the evidence path actually uses.

**Then the things that stop the learning.**

- **The brief contains the answer.** "Query `up` for pin-gateway services, then exclude the south district with an inequality matcher." The metric, the label, the operator, and the window are all handed over, so the player transcribes. Consider stating the operational need and the constraint and nothing else: "Vey wants a crew list for the gateways. Leave south out; that district is on the Assurance roster this week." The player then has to find `up`, find the district label, and choose the matcher, which is the whole lesson. (This pairs with item 3: a brief in the requester's voice naturally stops reading like a query plan.)
- **The hint ladder has one rung.** The second Orientation hint prints the full query shape; the Scaffold fills in the window; the Worked hint fills in the district. Three hints, two blanks. And at shift 35 the first three hints read "Open Registry.", "Use these numbered query shapes.", "Use these numbered scaffolds." Consider making the rungs actually differ: hint 1 restates the question and the result shape the answer should have (one series per district, a range, a count); hint 2 names the registry entries and the operator family with no expression ("this is a rate over a counter, summed by a label you keep"); hint 3 shows structure with genuine blanks (the function and the grouping, not the metric); hint 4 is the worked query. Since Call Marr costs clock time, each rung should be worth buying.
- **Failure verdicts don't say what failed.** Two sentences cover 385 of 586 outcomes and both are the failure messages: "The printouts do not prove the wider claim or its full scope" and "The selected title, conclusion, and action point to different findings." Neither mentions the result. Consider writing the failure from what the printouts showed: "Three series came back; the report names one facility." "The denominator covers north only; the conclusion speaks for every district." "The title says one door; the action authorizes the block." The clearance verdicts already do this ("West-03 returns a present series with value 0. Missing-99 returns no series.") and are the model.
- **The competing explanations aren't competing.** Every case's rival readings are the same two sentences with the case name dropped in. For a collector that may be down, the real rivals are "the collector failed", "the Pin is out of range", and "the record was never emitted", and choosing between them is the investigation. Two short authored rivals per case, or per shift, would replace the boilerplate.
- **The dilemma is invisible while you play.** The only number on the Personnel File is Standing, and in it honesty and the assured filing pay about the same, so during play lying never pays more than the truth. The four world counters the endings actually turn on (technical record, political control, humane service, evidence preserved) are never shown. Consider putting them on the Personnel File beside Standing, with the Ministry's names for them, so the player watches political control rise as they lie and evidence fall. Item 6 makes the standing side of this bite; this makes it visible.

**Then the shape of the campaign.**

- **75 cases share their worked queries with another case.** Only 150 distinct query sets exist across 196 cases. The queue-percentile case on shift 26 has the same hints and the same queries as a case on shift 18 and another on shift 33. Consider cutting the campaign to the cases that carry a distinct decision, or varying the duplicates by dataset so the same query gives a different answer that has to be read.
- **Concepts arrive in four avalanches and then nothing.** Eleven new concepts land on shift 10, ten on shift 18, ten on shift 19, six across shifts 34 and 35. Shifts 5 to 9, 12 to 17, 21 to 33, and 36 to 49 introduce nothing; that last stretch is fourteen shifts of repetition. Act IV adds no concept at all (see item 9). Consider spreading each avalanche over the dead stretch that follows it, one or two ideas per shift.
- **Every shift is four cases with the same budget.** No light day, no crisis day, no shift with one long case. Shift 41 leaves half an exploratory query per case, which is a demand for perfection rather than tension, while most shifts end with hours unused. Consider varying the shape with the story: a two-case day after a promotion, a six-case day when the paper says the presses are down, a single-case day for the perfect report.
- **Standing queries arrive late and unexplained, then pile up.** The desk shows "Standing queries 0/0" from shift 1 with one sentence of explanation. The mechanic appears four times in the first two-thirds of the game and eleven times in shifts 35 to 40. It is the best teaching idea in the design (a saved query scored on coverage, specificity, localization, timeliness, and cost against future data). Consider introducing one dimension at a time from Act II, and replacing the empty drawer with a line from Marr about what a standing query will do and how to earn the first slot.
- **The Registry's syntax page is one example and a list of internal feature names.** A player cannot discover that `irate` is unsupported before typing it. A one-line-per-function reference in the Registry, in the Ministry's voice, would be content rather than code and would remove a class of frustration.

A few smaller things from my own play: shift 1's paper gives the exact labels and the work order repeats them, so consider giving them only in the paper; the "1 of 1" ordinal runs into the worked-hint sentence; and titles ending in "s" break the question grammar. The engine-side notes (two small divergences from real Prometheus and Loki, and a list of unsupported functions) are in the learning auditor's report for whoever owns the engine.

## If you only take three

Fix the two bugs in item 11 first, since they are dead ends rather than design. Then items 1, 2, and 6. The form is where the game's central act happens, the paper is the world's face, and the standing economy is what makes lying necessary instead of optional. Item 4 is the cheapest of the rest and does the most for the feeling that the world answers back, and taking the answer out of the brief (item 11) is what turns the cases from transcription into investigation.

## How this was measured

Counts come from reading the campaign content directly and from a scripted playthrough of the prologue, the clearance shift, and shift 1 with zero console errors. A second auditor played shifts 1, 2, and 35 by hand and ran every authored worked answer through the game's grader to find the unsolvable cases; I confirmed the two causes and the evidence-count and duplicate-set numbers against the content myself. Distinct-string counts strip the case title before comparing. Nothing in the repo was changed for this audit except adding this document.
